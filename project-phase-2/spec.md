
# Digital Museum Exhibit Spec

## Project Overview
This digital museum exhibit explores how online culture has shaped the evolution of graphic design. It traces the shift from highly personal, user-controlled web aesthetics (GeoCities, MySpace, GIF buttons, Y2K graphics) to platform-driven minimalism, algorithm-influenced design systems, and contemporary subcultures that resist or parody corporate aesthetics (memes, vaporwave, brutalist web, anti-design).

## Audience
- Design students studying graphic and web design history
- Digital creators who use modern visual systems and want historical context
- Visitors familiar with early web aesthetics who want to see how they evolved

## Goals
- Show how online platforms and communities directly influenced visual design trends
- Highlight how algorithmic feeds and engagement metrics shaped branding and layout
- Contrast corporate minimalism with subcultural and anti-design movements
- Provide a visually engaging narrative that feels like a curated exhibit

## Content Scope (Exhibit Sections)
1. **Early Web Aesthetics (1995-2005)**
	- GeoCities pages, MySpace layouts, animated GIF buttons, Y2K graphics
	- Themes: personal identity, chaotic layouts, maximalism, DIY typography
2. **Platform Standardization (2006-2014)**
	- Rise of social platforms enforcing template-based design
	- Flat design and mobile-first branding
	- Themes: consistency, usability, feed optimization
3. **Algorithmic Influence (2015-Present)**
	- Engagement metrics affecting logo simplification and bold type
	- Scroll-optimized layouts and visual hierarchy for feeds
	- Themes: retention, attention, clarity at small sizes
4. **Subcultures and Anti-Design**
	- Memes, vaporwave, brutalist web design, anti-design
	- Themes: parody, resistance, nostalgia, remix culture

## Key Messages
- Online culture is not just a backdrop; it actively shapes graphic design trends.
- Design decisions are often responses to platform constraints and algorithms.
- Subcultures keep design experimental and push back on corporate standards.

## Exhibit Experience
- Multi-page structure with separate era pages
- Each era page uses its own visual skin (color, texture, motif)
- Guided tour navigation with a linear next/previous flow and transitions
- Each section includes:
	- Short intro text
	- A timeline card or micro-essay
	- Visual examples with captions and citations
	- Design Impact callout explaining how the visuals influenced design

## Visual Direction
- Split-era contrast: each era has a distinct palette, texture, and layout rhythm
- Museum-like framing with bold, era-specific visual accents

## Typography Direction
- Mixed-era pairing: one display font per era plus a consistent, clean body font
- Display type changes per era; body copy remains stable for readability

## Interaction Level
- High interaction with animated transitions between eras
- Interactive elements may include a timeline, a map of platforms, and a mini-playground

## Tone and Voice
- Curatorial and academic
- Concise, explanatory captions paired with clear citations

## Media Priorities
- Mix of screenshots with captions plus selective interactive pieces

## Accessibility
- Reduced motion option for animations and transitions
