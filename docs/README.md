# Museum Exhibit: Algorithmic Aesthetics

Algorithmic Aesthetics is a digital museum exhibit about how online culture shaped graphic design. It follows four eras: early web aesthetics, platform standardization, algorithmic influence, and subcultures/anti-design.

The site is designed to feel like a guided exhibition rather than a blog or a landing page. Visitors move through a curatorial sequence of images, commentary, object labels, and sources that explain how digital platforms changed visual culture.

## Orchestration Process

I tried to follow the spec structure we'd been doing this semester. In the beginning I created the website just with basic HTML/CSS and created a spec that defined what kind of exhibit I wanted to make, then I had the spec divided into sprint documents and implemented them one at a time, verifying each pass with linting, typechecking, content checks, and build validations. I created the planning folder to hold the planned, active, and completed sprints. 

I did what Professor Williams suggested and took my project and had the best ideas taken from it to be worked into a new version. In my case, I felt like it didn't do that well of a job taking what was best and I had to do a lot of tweaking.

After Professor Williams provided us with his example project, I imported his work into my project under an example directory, had copilot analyze and take the tech stack, structure, and best practices to rework into a new version of my project. I followed the same structure of having a spec document divided into multiple sprints and implementing it one at a time and making tweaks in between. 

One thing I would do differently next time is to not have the spec create each sprint all at once, and instead create and implement each sprint one by one. I learned that too late into my project, so I took note to do it next time.

## Style, Brand, and Method of Persuasion

I tried to approach a grunge style, but also have each era page simulate the feeling of that historical period. I tried to apply a museum-like feel to the pages to create a guided,curated experience. 

I applied Cialdini's Authority principle as my main method of persuasion. The authority from my website comes from the clear object labels, citations, curational framing, and consistent structure across the exhibit.
